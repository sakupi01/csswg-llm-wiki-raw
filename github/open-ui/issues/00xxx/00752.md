As the research says, some ui kits/design systems support exclusive accordions.  However, I'd like to point out that there isn't actually total agreement on what that means in detail.  It seems that there are two, maybe even three different mental models/variants of exclusivity in the wild.  That is, in an "exclusive" accordion, is it possible to have _none of them_ expanded?  Believe it or not - there isn't agreement!  A subtle variant of that is: Is that valid only as an initial state, or can you also collapse the active one at any time.

FAST, for example[1] in the single select version _appears_ to follow a very common "Highlander" interpretation of exclusive: [There can be only 1... Ever](https://explore.fast.design/components/fast-accordion ). Of course, I could be wrong about this[1]

Fluent has many modes, but the one under the heading ["open"](https://react.fluentui.dev/?path=/docs/components-accordion--default#open-items) would seem to me to be the subtle variant -- the example seems to imply it is possible to have none selected initially, but there it is impossible to gain create that state. Again, I could be wrong about this[1]

Anyway: I think this is actually kind of critical to discuss since the proposal is about [exclusive mode](https://open-ui.org/components/accordion.explainer/) and it is drawing exceptionally close to radio buttons.  I always use "like radio buttons" and "like checkboxes" when describing things too, but I think we need to get into the details a bit more.  Radio buttons inherently allow "nothing to be selected" and have a very particular "you can't unselect through direct interactions once selected". If those are kind of the same things we copy for single select accordion this would be a lot easier - but the only thing I could find that seems to me (again, I could be wrong[1]) to work like that on quick examination was Fluent (which is, I believe being disontinued).



---
[1] One of the challenges that we noted in our tabs research is kind of "generally true" and that is that there are very few orgs making these based on careful and ongoing research - a lot of what we use is unfortunately recreated over and over by many different people implementing what they think is the same thing. The interesting bit is that it's a lot like evolution, we wind up with lots of minor variations through just simple imperfect copies.  It's very difficult to know which of these are valuable, harmful, intentional, etc.  I am even a bit unsure that what I said about FAST _never_ allowing nothing to be selected is true... It has me thinking that we should really probably be linking issues in the repos of these systems, asking them to confirm that that _is_ correct and inviting them to explain why/join the discussion. Even if we never get a standard out of it, at least there is an opportunity for people to say "oh, wow... yeah, we just didn't think of that" or something.
