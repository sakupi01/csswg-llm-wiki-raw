Our the initial [Parts and Concepts](https://open-ui.org/components/tabs.research.parts) and the bulk of our [A Survey and Anaylsis of Markup Approaches](https://open-ui.org/components/tabs.research.markup) attempted to survey and report information without specifics of proposals as we saw a number of things that would require more thought.  Toward the end of the survey we did provide some thought and recommendations which haven't had a lot of discussion in the calls, but they were also a little fuzzy.

Since then we've also continued to think on this and I [published a blog post](https://bkardell.com/blog/SpicySections.html) explaining a considerably more concrete idea that several of us have been exploring (and why we didn't make other choices), including a custom element demonstrating the thrust of the idea itself - that maybe some of these 'components' are really better as affordances grantable to certain kinds of content (more similar to how scrollbars work today).  Lots of details tbd there if we go that way, but we believe this splits problems for discussion in a way we don't have to have all of the answers to discuss the key points - it could (we believe) dovetail nicely with the work presented by @tabatkins and @mirisuzanne a few weeks ago.

We have asked for developer feedback in a few channels - so far we have heard only positive feedback, but it's early... Given that this is kind of unique, isn't very common in UI kits already as a markup approach and isn't even unique to tabs we'd love to present/discuss what people's feelings are as we try to solidify something into an explainer/actual proposal we can drive forward.

## @hidde — 2021-07-15

As a developer I really like this, and can see it work well, even if my designer counterpart comes up with affordances that look and work quite different from [Brian's demo](https://codepen.io/bkardell/pen/VwpJGGL?editors=1100). From the looks of it this idea allows for all the customisations.

It also makes sense to me semantically to group content into _x_ sections and have them display as tabs, linear content or collapsibles, depending on viewport. 

Random thought: I wonder if there would be appetite for a user setting around this. Maybe one user wants to always see “content groups” as tabs, another wants to always see them as collapsibles, and yet another may not care as they use a screenreader mostly with their screen turned off. If authors mark this up consistently, could browsers honour such user preferences?
