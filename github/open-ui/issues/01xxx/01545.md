Most HTML controls use `click` as their activation mechanism, regardless of how they're activated. For example, moving the selection with the arrow keys in a radio group fires a click, as does pressing Space on a button.

I wonder if `<option>` activation should follow the same pattern.

Currently, in `appearance: base`, Chrome activates/selects options through various events depending on the interaction method. In `appearance: auto`, both Chrome and Firefox already fire click events.

This would mean that keyboard selection would also fire a trusted click, and calling `preventDefault()` on the click event would prevent the option from being selected or unselected directly.

One use case would be easily soft-disabling an option:

```js
select.addEventListener('click', (event) => {
  if (event.target.closest('option[aria-disabled="true"]')) {
    event.preventDefault();
  }
});
```

Mouse drag selection is a bit tricky because in Chrome an option can be selected on `mouseup` even if the eventual click target is somewhere else on the page.

Perhaps with click-based activation we could require the click target to be the `<select>` or one of its descendants, and fire a second click on the option itself, similar to how `<label>` forwards activation.
I doubt anyone relies on selecting options by starting a drag click outside the `<select>` anyway.

Also, `accesskey` already works, just mentioning it as another activation method that fires a click event.

Yeah it wasn't ideal to move the activation behavior for option elements from click to mouseup, but it seemed necessary at the time in order to enable the click and drag gesture, and it didn't seem like authors would need to instrument the activation behavior at all since it just does what its supposed to do on its own, and authors can already listen to the change event. Thanks for the use case example.

When doing a click and drag, the click event is fired on the nearest ancestor of the click down and click up targets, which in this case is the select element itself. We could change this as a special case for clicking and dragging to choose an option. @dbaron did we ever implement such a thing for menu elements?
