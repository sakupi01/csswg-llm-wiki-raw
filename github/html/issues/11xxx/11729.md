### What problem are you trying to solve?

Some of us over in Open UI have been designing the new menu elements proposal ([explainer](https://open-ui.org/components/menu.explainer/)), which is a suite of new HTML elements designed to fulfill the [menu](https://w3c.github.io/aria/#menu) and [menubar](https://w3c.github.io/aria/#menubar) roles / pattern. Please see the [APG pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/menubar/) for more details, which served as some initial motivation and inspiration for our proposal.

Our goal is to produce a set of menu primitives with sensible built-in keyboard navigability, accessibility, and activation defaults, as we've observed this pattern widely across the web, with each instance varying slightly in all forms of behavior, and many of which have very poor accessibility characteristics. Our proposal builds off of recent primitives such as popovers and anchor positioning, and all of the design details to date have been collected in the explainer: https://open-ui.org/components/menu.explainer/. Please take a look!

Our goal is eventual inclusion in HTML, so we'd love to propose stage 1, and discuss this at the WHATNOT meeting scheduled for 2025-10-09.

## @dbaron — 2026-06-16

A slightly longer update on the question of nesting versus idrefs (cc @annevk @smaug----):

We [discussed](https://github.com/openui/open-ui/issues/1456#issuecomment-4683985964) the issue at the Open UI meeting last week, and I since (as I said above) added a [section](https://open-ui.org/components/menu.explainer/#should-the-menu-elements-use-id-references-or-nesting-for-submenu-relationships) on this topic to the explainer.

As part of adding that section I went through the [existing libraries](https://open-ui.org/components/menu.research/) documented by Open UI library research, and came to a conclusion that disagreed with my previous understanding of the research on existing libraries.  While many libraries express menus as JSON rather than as markup (which I think makes them substantially less relevant to the question, although perhaps not irrelevant) and a bunch of the libraries don't appear to document a way to do submenus, I found three libraries that both described menus with markup and supported submenus.  All three used a nested structure.  Two of the three ([Material Web](https://github.com/material-components/material-web/blob/main/docs/components/menu.md) and [React Spectrum](https://react-spectrum.adobe.com/v3/Menu.html)) avoided nested interactives ("Option B") while the third ([Semantic UI](https://semantic-ui.com/collections/menu.html)) took the approach with nested interactive elements ("Option C").

Given the various discussions (in WHATNOT and OpenUI) and that additional research my current plan is to start work on updating the proposal (prototype, explainer, and HTML spec PR) to an approach that uses nesting, along the lines of Option B in https://github.com/openui/open-ui/issues/1456, meaning something like:
```html
<menubar>
  <submenu>
    <menulabel>File</menulabel>
    <menulist>
      <menuitem>New</menuitem>
      <menuitem>Open</menuitem>
    </menulist>
  </submenu>
  <submenu>
    <menulabel>Edit</menulabel>
    <menulist>
      <menuitem>Cut</menuitem>
      <menuitem>Copy</menuitem>
      <menuitem>Paste</menuitem>
    </menulist>
  </submenu>
</menubar>
```

This update would mean dropping the commands-related parts of the proposal, but continuing to make menulists be popovers.

I'm interested in feedback on this plan -- and preferably sooner than later -- since what this plan likely means is that I'm going to spend the next few weeks revising the prototype, explainer, and spec, and then try to resume the discussion about moving this proposal to Stage 2.

## @dbaron — 2026-06-29

I think it's worth noting that one of the strong pieces of feedback in favor of a grouping element (e.g., in https://github.com/openui/open-ui/issues/1189#issuecomment-4814143764) is that it's bad authoring practice to do anything other than have a consecutive run of checkable or radio menuitems.  In particular, a group of radio or checkbox menu items should not be separated by other menuitems or anything else.  So having a parent element that controls radio/checkbox state helps push authors toward following good practices.

Another rationale is that there was a desire for a rendered caption/legend for a group of radio or checkbox menuitems, and `<fieldset>` + `<legend>` provide this somewhat naturally.  (Note that I haven't yet worked on or investigated rendering aspects of this in Chromium's current prototype implementation, and I was holding off until the discussion of whether we'd actually use `<fieldset>` was sorted out... so odds are it doesn't yet look reasonable.)
