Reported in crbug.com/817996.

```
<meter value=3 min=0 max=4 low=2 high=3 optimum=4></meter>
<meter value=2 min=0 max=4 low=2 high=3 optimum=1></meter>
```
Chrome, Edge, and Safari show green (optimum) meters, and Firefox shows yellow (suboptimal) meters.

["UA requirements for regions of the gauge" paragraph in the spec](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/form-elements.html#the-meter-element)
> If the optimum point is equal to the low boundary or the high boundary, or anywhere in between them, then the region between the low and high boundaries of the gauge must be treated as the optimum region, and the low and high parts, if any, must be treated as suboptimal. Otherwise, if the optimum point is less than the low boundary, then the region between the minimum value and the low boundary must be treated as the optimum region, the region from the low boundary up to the high boundary must be treated as a suboptimal region, and the remaining region must be treated as an even less good region. Finally, if the optimum point is higher than the high boundary, then the situation is reversed; the region between the high boundary and the maximum value must be treated as the optimum region, the region from the high boundary down to the low boundary must be treated as a suboptimal region, and the remaining region must be treated as an even less good region.

This paragraph contains ambiguous words like *between* and *from* to define regions. Firefox seems to assume the optimum region is exclusive, and other browsers assume the optimum region is inclusive.

## @da2x — 2022-03-11

> Firefox seems to assume the optimum region is exclusive, and other browsers assume the optimum region is inclusive.

This isn’t entirely correct. Firefox always assume its exclusive. However, Safari and Chrome assumes the boundary between the low and medium regions are exclusive, and the medium and high regions are inclusive. As shown in this table:

![Browser compatibility table](https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/1102886/157828480-159c8c73-9da9-46a2-86b5-45f6affe0251.png)

However, the spec as it’s currently written suggest the low and high attributes should be inclusive. (An attribute named low defining a region called low should logically be included in said region.) So, **no browser is correct.** I’ve just written [an article arguing for this interpretation](https://www.ctrl.blog/entry/html-meter-segment-boundaries.html).
