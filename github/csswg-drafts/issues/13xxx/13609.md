## @fantasai — 2026-07-24

Here's an attempted reformulation of this:

Form controls should feel usable out-of-the-box: a typical author shouldn't find them distasteful to use in production as-is.

Translation: While `appearance: base` might not perfectly match the website's graphic branding, it shouldn't feel like an author _needs_ to fix the styling because it's broken in some way or feels off or lacks a baseline level of polish (good alignment, pleasant iconography, etc.). It should feel professional and complete, even if unopinionated and uninteresting.

<!-- comment id=5071792134 author=mfreed7 created=2026-07-24T15:56:30Z url=https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13609#issuecomment-5071792134 -->
## @mfreed7 — 2026-07-24

Generally, I'm quite afraid of the slippery slope of "distasteful", "feels off", "lacks polish", or "pleasant". Those are all design aesthetic related, and design aesthetic (almost by definition somehow?) changes over time. A majority of people look back at designs from 5-10 years ago and think "that looks so old and unpleasant". I want to make sure we're not trying to lock `appearance:base` into being an always-up-to-date design system. It isn't, and practically *can't be*. We only update these things once every few decades. The `appearance:base` styles are the *substrate* for design systems.

There's another reading of the proposal above, which is that the form controls shouldn't **confuse the user** by being broken in some way. That, also, can be a slippery slope, since how controls function can change over time. But this rate of change is quite a bit slower than the design aesthetics of those controls. And also this is the one that is likely to cause bugs/problems, rather than "just" developer distaste and added work to update CSS to keep up with the times.

Can we re-formulate the criteria to mention "brokenness", and not "distaste"? And perhaps instead of "**feel** usable", "**be** usable by a majority of users" or similar?

<!-- comment id=5097091935 author=tabatkins created=2026-07-27T21:36:59Z url=https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13609#issuecomment-5097091935 -->
## @tabatkins — 2026-07-27

I agree that the biggest thing we need to worry about is it being *usable* and not looking broken/confusing. It needs to look *okay*, but without employing too much styling that'll need to be reverted when authors *do* style them into their page style. If authors end up needing to effectively apply a reset to their `appearance:base` inputs just to start from a reasonable canvas when doing their own work, I think we'll have failed.

That said, I'm definitely open to seeing what changes might be proposed! I think there's a lot of wiggle room under these constraints to still do something that looks nice.

I also think it's worth pointing out that the resolution this followed seems to not have stuck? (Border radius change on select) In which case it suggests that this idea of "distasteful" is even harder to pin down.

Fwiw I largely agree with Mason, I think appearance base should work and be usable and understandable by users. But I think pinning the principles to something like authors would ship unstyled is chasing an impossible. No one is going to actually do that and that's fine. Base appearance should be exactly a base. Something solid they can target and build atop. Interoperability is imo the main aim of this.
