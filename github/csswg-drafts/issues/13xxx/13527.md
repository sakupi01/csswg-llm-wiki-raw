The double border between the ::picker() and the button looks slightly unpleasant:

<img width="440" height="208" alt="Image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/758765b2-7773-48b7-90f6-e98811aa4486" />

Some options to fix it:
1) add spacing on top of the popover (not my preferred option given it's one more property override), so the picker is spaced away from the button.
2) add negative margin around the popover (not my preferred option given it breaks when you start to have different border-widths on the button, and it's also annoying to override)
2) anchor the popover to the padding-box of the button instead of the border-box (@fantasai told me there was an extension to anchor positioning that would allow that).

Personally in favor of 3 given it's the least pain for developers. I don't know how this would look like in the UA sheet though (potentially `position-area: padding-box block-end inline-start` or something)

## @mfreed7 — 2026-02-19

I'm really afraid we're on a very slippery slope towards a very opinionated design system in the UA stylesheet for customizable select. The [design principle](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-forms-1/#basic-appearance-principles) is "The controls are recognizable and usable on their own without additional styles." I'd argue (perhaps strongly) that nothing about the double border makes this unrecognizable, or unusable. E.g. the OP says this "looks slightly unpleasant" which is a design choice, not something that affects usability. I'd lean more heavily on this point of the principles: "They do not have a strong voice & tone of their own, and are visually as simple as possible."

## @sorvell — 2026-03-05

> The [design principle](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-forms-1/#basic-appearance-principles) is "The controls are recognizable and usable on their own without additional styles."

The principles are great as a guide but clearly there's a need for interpretation and judgment.

> The styles are consistent across controls…in look & feel.

There's also this, and I suggest that if rounding and borders are used at all, they should be consistent and apply to the control, as a whole.

This leads me to [the proposal here](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13520#issuecomment-4001368256).
