In [last week's discussion](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/12594#issuecomment-3530564088) of CSS navigation / route matching, @jakearchibald raised the issue of being able to query whether a navigation was back or forward.

I *think* @jakearchibald's use case relates to the idea that a developer wants a design where the UI has some sort of "physical" (in animation) representation of the browser's session history, most likely through side-to-side movement.  (@jakearchibald, is that correct?)  I think this is different from the use cases we were initially focusing on, where the motion of an animation from a list of items to the details of one item, or the animation from details to list, depends on the "from" and "to" of the navigation in order to represent the movement of the single item between being the focus of the UI to being a single item within a list, and that motion doesn't depend on the type of navigation.  But maybe there are also other use cases for knowing this -- which may or may not differ in which exact distinctions they want to make.  It would be useful to raise additional use cases here.

Then there's the question of what we want to query.  The space of possibilities is a bit larger than just back/forward.  I think a good high-level summary of the cases is the [`NavigationType`](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/nav-history-apis.html#navigationtype) enum in the HTML spec, which has `push`, `replace`, `reload`, and `traverse`.  Note that back and forward *both* fall into the "traverse" category (where typical browser [back](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/nav-history-apis.html#dom-history-back) and [forward](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/nav-history-apis.html#dom-history-forward) buttons correspond to a delta of -1 or +1, though they often provide additional drop-down UI for deltas further from 0).

Then the question is, which things do we need to distinguish?
* It seems like exposing the 4-way distinction in the `NavigationType` enum above is probably a good baseline.
* I think @jakearchibald's use case wants to know (for the `traverse`) case whether the delta is positive or negative -- and maybe even exactly what it is?
* Do the `push` and `replace` cases need to distinguish between navigation and `history.pushState()`/`history.replaceState()`?
* Do we need more in addition to that?  Or less?
