For `center` alignment, matching the [center of the smallest possible virtual circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumscribed_circle) (or possibly ellipse) surrounding the box to align with the same center of its alignment container may give better visual results than using the horizontal and vertical center of rectangular boxes. I propose a new positional alignment keyword `circular-center` for this.

This alignment is particularly useful for non-rectangular boxes as specified in [css-shapes], but the level-1 draft of this module only considers custom shapes for floating boxes. For rectangular boxes and shapes, `circular-center` equals `center`.

## @tabatkins — 2024-03-05

I'm not sure I understand what's being requested here. For rectangles, the circumcircle has the same center as the rectangle itself.

The medium post you've linked to describes the use of a circumcircle around the *non-rectangular shape*, which can definitely give a different answer than the center of the shape's bounding box. But we only have rectangles to work with.

<!-- comment id=1978034111 author=Crissov created=2024-03-05T06:11:35Z url=https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/1849#issuecomment-1978034111 -->
## @Crissov — 2024-03-05

Yes, that is why I referenced the Shapes module. Its `shape-outside` currently only affects the [_float area_](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-shapes/#float-area) of float elements (defaulting to the [margin box](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-box/#margin-box)) and not the [_alignment subject_](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-align/#alignment-subject) (also defaulting to the margin box). This would need to change for `circular-center` to differ from `center`.
