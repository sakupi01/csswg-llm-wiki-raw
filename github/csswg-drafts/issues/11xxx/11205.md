How should `block-step-size` interact with `aspect-ratio`?

Suggestion:
- If the logical width depends on the logical height, adjust the width accordingly.
- If the logical height depends on the logical width, tweak only the height.

## @jensimmons — 2025-01-13

I think what authors will expect is for:
- the small square will grow in both directions, maintaining aspect ratio
- the full-width image only grows in one direction, not maintaining aspect ratio 

… but only when `block-step-insert: content-box`.

If `block-step-insert` is set to `margin-box` or `padding-box` then the aspect ratio will be ignored in all cases. AKA, the square hamburger photo will stay exactly the same size, and just have more margin/padding above/below. 

Perhaps the rubric could be something like this: 
- if `block-step-insert: content-box` 
- then attempt to adjust the `auto` dimension in order to maintain `aspect-ratio`
- if there is not enough room to adjust height/width because of the sizing of the container, then don't, instead ignore the aspect ratio

<!-- comment id=2587815071 author=jensimmons created=2025-01-13T18:02:53Z url=https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/11205#issuecomment-2587815071 -->
## @jensimmons — 2025-01-13

Conversely, there could be a situation where the author wants to make the content (image or something else) be:
- 16 x 9 aspect ratio
- 50% of the width of the container
- and nudge the height just a little to step to the next 1lh, _without_ making the content wider 

The "rubric" in the comment just before this one would not allow for this situation.

We could instead simply always ignore `aspect-ratio`. 

<!-- comment id=2587881830 author=jensimmons created=2025-01-13T18:22:03Z url=https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/11205#issuecomment-2587881830 -->
## @jensimmons — 2025-01-13

Perhaps both use cases — wanting content to grow in the inline direction & wanting it to not grow — can be handled at the same time through `block-step-insert`:

- `block-step-insert: content` — content _is_ resized in the inline direction to maintain aspect ratio (if there's enough space to do so)
- `block-step-insert: padding` — [maybe content is resized in the inline direction, maybe not]
- `block-step-insert:margin` — content is _not_ resized in the inline direction to maintain aspect ratio, extra space is added to the block margins & the resulting margin box does not conform to the aspect ratio
