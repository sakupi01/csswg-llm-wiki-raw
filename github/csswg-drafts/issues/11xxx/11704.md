(Originally brought up in https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/11558)

As a naive web author, I would like to do something like:
- Show a carousel of images in `dynamic-range-limit: constrained` HDR,
- except for one currently-selected child image that can "punch through" as `high`/`no-limit`;
- And on the side I'd like to show an iframe with ads, but force it down to `standard`, and prevent it from ever going any higher.

Could there be a permission policy that can be applied to iframes, e.g.: `allow="extended-dynamic-range"`,  `allow="constrained-extended-dynamic-range"`? (Or something more flexible with options that match current/future `dynamic-range-limit` values.)

Other ideas?

## @kyouhei-horizumi — 2026-07-08

I support allowing an embedding document to impose an irreversible dynamic-range limit on untrusted embedded content, such as advertisements, through Permissions Policy or an equivalent mechanism.

I tested the same HDR image inside a cross-origin iframe. Even when `dynamic-range-limit: standard` was applied to the `iframe` element in the embedding page, the HDR image inside the iframe was still displayed brighter than an SDR white reference in the embedding document.

On X, profile images are rendered with `dynamic-range-limit` and `filter: brightness(1)`, which suppresses HDR output. However, iframe widgets that do not apply equivalent mitigation may cause HDR images to appear much brighter than the surrounding UI on the embedding page, making them stand out in an unusually distracting way.

As HDR advertising becomes more common, this additional luminance could be abused by advertisements, fake warning messages, and other untrusted content to attract disproportionate attention. Before so-called “HDR spam” becomes a widespread problem, embedding documents should have a way to impose a limit such as `standard` that cannot be overridden by child documents or nested iframes.
