## @frivoal — 2019-10-17

More details about my logic:
1. Firefox's behavior at 6ch is bad because a `pre-wrap` space that should be in the middle of the line disappears (the green one)
2. Chrome's behavior at 6ch is bad because text overflows (`ا`) even though there are wrapping opportunities on the line
3. Safari and Edge are both good at 6ch.
4. Safari is broken at 7ch because it fails to reorder the green space
5. Edge, Chrome, and Firefox are all good at 7ch.
6. Safari, Chrome, and Firefox's behaviors are all acceptable at 8ch, the difference is that Safari and Firefox chose to collapse the advance width of the hanging space that overflows (the yellow one), as allowed but not required by the last sentence of 4.1.3 point 4 bullet 2.
7. Edge's behavior at 7ch is bad because it wraps an end-of-line space that ought to hang (the yellow one), as per 4.1.3 point 4 bullet 2.
8. All browsers are good at 8ch.

Most of the criteria underpinning that logic were already in the spec, but not all. The proposed PR adds those of 1. and 2.

## @jfkthame — 2019-10-24

> I think we should match to UAX#9. In 3.4 Reordering Resolved Levels, the rule [L1](http://unicode.org/reports/tr9/#L1) defines how to handle trailing spaces.

This seems to imply that the Safari result is correct for 7ch ("trailing whitespace will appear at the visual end of the line", which means the green space should remain at the right-hand edge, not be reordered adjacent to the red one). But then ISTM the Firefox result for 6ch makes sense: the line-break is the same as the 7ch case, but the trailing green space (at the right-hand edge) is allowed to hang or be suppressed.
