The [color-mix function](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-color-5/#colormix) takes an optional colorspace. The default is LCH. The available options are `sRGB` (and the polar forms of sRGB, `HSL` and `HWB`), the newly-introduced predefined RGB spaces `display-p3`, `a98-rgb`, `prophoto-rgb`, `rec2020`, and the two forms of Lab `Lab` and `LCH`.

 * `Lab` and `LCH` are perceptually uniform, but not linear-light
 * the RGB forms are gamma-corrected and thus, not linear-light either
 * the polar forms of sRGB are the usual hot mess and certainly neither  perceptually uniform, nor linear-light

Correct color mixing (what you would get by shining two colored spotlights on a stage and them moving them so the beams overlap; or displaying a tiny checkerboard of two colors on a screen and squinting) requires a linear-light colorspace.

[linear-light sRGB](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-color-4/#linear-light-sRGB) is defined in the spec, and is one option. (This is the default space for SVG filters, for example). But it is limited to the sRGB gamut.

linear-light versions of the other RGB spaces could be defined, but also have gamut limitations.

Any linear-light colorspace can be converted to any other by 3x3 matrix multiplication, and the results of interpolation will be identical in the two spaces. There exists an unbounded, infinite-gamut linear-light colorspace, CIE XYZ. Calculating XYZ is part of the conversion from any RGB space to Lab and LCH. Interpolating in XYZ will give the same result as interpolating in linear-sRGB (or linear-anyRGB) for colors inside the RGB gamuts.

Thus, `color-mix` should add an XYZ option to the other colorspaces, for when you want the actual physical result of color mixing.
