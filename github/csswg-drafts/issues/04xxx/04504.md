The current implementations of `text-decoration-skip-ink` in Firefox and Safari skip over the inside of glyphs leaving gaps within the underlines.

@litherum [mentioned in #4493](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/4493#issuecomment-551257774) that he implemented it this way because "little bits of underlines inside bowls of letters like lowercase g [...] were too ugly to ship." and in https://bugzil.la/1575383 the opinions for having underlines displayed within glyphs were ambivalent.

[Text Decoration 3](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-text-decor-3/#line-decoration) currently has a note about this saying:

> Note, this specification intentionally does not mandate a particular method for “following the shape” of the glyph so that UAs can take appropriate measures to handle aesthetic and performance considerations.

I believe in some cases it does make sense to show the underlines in the inner parts of the glyphs, especially in combination with negative `text-underline-offset`s and/or bigger font sizes.

Example:

Showing underline within glyphs:
![underlining without skipping inner parts of glyphs](https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/958943/68546371-cec29f00-03d5-11ea-9975-52b023a78b62.png)

Skipping underline within characters:
![underlining with skipping inner parts of glyphs](https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/958943/68546396-00d40100-03d6-11ea-94c3-4f43c3cf2e1f.png)

See https://jsfiddle.net/SebastianZ/qprzf15b/ or for browsers not supporting `text-underline-offset` yet https://jsfiddle.net/SebastianZ/pe8rf4a3/.

## @jfkthame — 2019-11-10

It's not a general solution for all cases, obviously, but for simple examples an effect like this could be "faked" by turning off the current skip-ink behavior, and then stroking the text with the background color; try https://codepen.io/jfkthame/pen/mddjVXp in Firefox.

Whether this is worth browsers implementing "properly" as a built-in skip-ink behavior seems a bit questionable to me; I'm having a hard time thinking of a case where using a large font-size and negative underline-offset would be sensible design! (I do have more sympathy for the case of Cyrillic Д, and no doubt there are other characters with similar issues.)
