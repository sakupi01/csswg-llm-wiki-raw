In [section 2](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-scrollbars-1/#scrollbar-color-properties), the terms "scrollbar-track" and "scrollbar-face" are used without defining what these are. The terms used to describe the components of a scrollbar vary from platform to platform, so the ones chosen here must be defined to ensure that all readers are on the same page.

Presumably the track is the main bar background area; the "face" is the more confusing term. Is that the draggable widget (which I have called an "elevator" since sometime in the mid-1980s despite various other terms floating past me over the years)? Does the face include the arrows at the end(s) of the scroll bar? If so, is it the background of the arrow's box, the foreground? Or is it the scrollbar's bounding box? Or something else entirely? Or is it intended to be up to the client to decide what to do with these values?

<!-- comment id=403669030 author=upsuper created=2018-07-10T01:22:58Z url=https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/2898#issuecomment-403669030 -->
## @upsuper — 2018-07-10

The dragging area is the "face" and the area serving as a track of the dragging area is "track". And everything else intends to be platform-dependent, like whether arrows should take "face" color or "track" color or neither (e.g. no arrows at all, or use some color derived from either or both specified colors).

<!-- comment id=403772407 author=SelenIT created=2018-07-10T10:13:00Z url=https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/2898#issuecomment-403772407 -->
## @SelenIT — 2018-07-10

Some of the documented examples (e.g. [TweetDeck](https://github.com/webcompat/web-bugs/issues/9791#issuecomment-403218773). mentioned [in the rationale that pseudo-elements are not needed](https://www.w3.org/wiki/Css-scrollbars#TweetDeck), but apparently under-examined back then) also display intent to control the shape (rounded corners) and minimum size of the dragging area. Are there any chances that the spec will take these aspects into account?

FWIW, the word "thumb" is also used in browser-specific pseudo-elements name for the quite similar part of the value slider (`<input type="range">`).
