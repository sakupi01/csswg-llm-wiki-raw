When using `content-visibility: auto` some of the content below the fold may have a temporary `height`, and therefor scroll position, and consequently `ScrollTimeline.currentTime` will be temporary, until scrolled to that content and will result in a jump in scroll progress, probably resulting in a jump in both `currentTime` and animation's progress.
I'm assuming this is expected behavior.
Is this something that can somehow be avoided/optimized by the UA? Or worked around by the author?

<!-- comment id=1200454092 author=ydaniv created=2022-07-31T16:11:56Z url=https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/7545#issuecomment-1200454092 -->
## @ydaniv — 2022-07-31

I'm kind of feeling this will be a foot-gun that can be avoided.
Perhaps it's safe to assume that the scrolling experience in this case should win over the loading experience - which is only optimized here.
Is it possible for the UA to detect whether an optimized element is a descendant of a `ScrollTimeline.source`, and in that case opt-out of optimization?
