I was looking at the third test of [this WPT](https://wpt.fyi/results/scroll-animations/scroll-timelines/scroll-timeline-invalidation.html): https://github.com/web-platform-tests/wpt/blob/fb587ab2ec2a085f3e1227773d7d69db79c79691/scroll-animations/scroll-timelines/scroll-timeline-invalidation.html#L98-L140

That test passes in WebKit but not Blink or Gecko. But I'm a bit confused as to what makes it sound. Looking at it, it comes from @graouts in https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=284546 / https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/commit/3da5ec4d32bde7cc2bcd0f51f14c17175722ef75.

I get the intent of the test, which is that, since nothing for that particular timeline hasn't changed, it shouldn't need re-sampling. However that works in WebKit just by [checking the max-scroll-range](https://searchfox.org/wubkat/rev/d221d71a6a560cb45bb3e9b23e526b6bcfa9e4b3/Source/WebCore/animation/ScrollTimeline.cpp#313-314) IIUC, but that doesn't seem sound to me. For `ViewTimeline` for example, the ranges might depend on the subject size / insets etc.

So the spec says in https://drafts.csswg.org/scroll-animations-1/#event-loop says that only just-created timelines and (view) timelines whose named ranges change are added to the stale set... That can't be right? At least it seems #8694 intended to deal with "the scroller grew" or "the subject changed size". And the test would just fail if I implement that to the letter.

So my question is I guess, what "stale timeline" definition do we want? It seems we want to include basically "any timeline whose current time or ranges would change as a result of the style and layout update"... But at that point I think the test expectation should change maybe?

Or am I misreading this badly? Note that I'm not the most familiar with scroll timelines so I _might_ just be reading it wrong somehow?
