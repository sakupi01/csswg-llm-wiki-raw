A note in *10.4.1. Argument Ranges* says:
> The tangent function is discontinuous at its asymptotes

This is false, despite widespread belief, and even some curricula. A function can be continuous or discontinuous only at a point in its domain. Tangent is no more discontinuous at π/2 than at tangent (no mathematical function can be applied to itself) or at Krzysztof Maczyński vel ByteEater (that's me; despite being a member of infinitely many sets, I'm pretty sure the tangent's domain isn't one of them).

I suggest saying instead that tangent has no limit (not even `infinity` or `-infinity`) at those points. (If a domain is viewed as a subset of a space – the (extended) real line in this case – limits may be considered at all points of its closure.)
