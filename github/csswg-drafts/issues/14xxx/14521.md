Currently, [css-color-4 requires UAs to implement one of three GMAs](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-color/#gamut-mapping): MINDE, EdgeSeeker, Raytrace.

However, what we _actually_ care about is not whether UAs use a specific algorithm, but that a good output color is used that preserves lightness (primarily) and hue (secondarily) almost exactly, and does not reduce chroma more than is necessary ([as happens with naive chroma reduction](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-color/#GM-excessive-reduction)).

We could compute the maximum L and H errors for these three algorithms, and specify that UAs must instead preserve these thresholds, plus something to ensure C is not reduced more than is needed.

Or, perhaps a hybrid: either use one of these three GMAs, or feel free to use your own as long as it follows these requirements.

I’m thinking this could give UAs some freedom, while preserving the spirit of gamut mapping, and potentially making it easier for UAs to implement.

@svgeesus disagrees, so I’ll let him speak on his rationale.
