https://drafts.csswg.org/css-align/#justify-block

> - [`normal`](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-align/#valdef-justify-self-normal) Behavior: The box lays out according to the default rules for block layout (see [CSS2.1§10.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/CSS2/visudet.html#Computing_widths_and_margins)).
> - Other Details: The [automatic size](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-sizing-3/#automatic-size) of a [block-level box](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-display-4/#block-level-box) whose [justify-self](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-align/#propdef-justify-self) is not [normal](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-align/#valdef-justify-self-normal) is equivalent to [fit-content](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-sizing-4/#valdef-column-width-fit-content) (rather than e.g. [stretch](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-sizing-3/#valdef-width-stretch), as is typical for a [non-replaced](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-display-4/#non-replaced) block-level box).

My interpretation was that both replaced elements and [widgets](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-ui-4/#widget) (a.k.a. kinda-replaced elements) do not stretch with `justify-self: normal`.

However, turns out that `<meter>` and `<progress>` do stretch on Blink and WebKit (but not on Gecko):

```html
<!DOCTYPE html>
<style>body > * { display: block; width: auto }</style>
<meter></meter>
<progress></progress>
```

So, are they actually widgets? I think so:
 - They definitely look like widgets
 - The are mentioned in https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/rendering.html#widgets
 - They obey `width` despite `display: inline`

But then, is it the case that some block-level widgets still stretch with `justify-self: normal`? Or are Blink&WebKit just wrong?
