https://drafts.csswg.org/scroll-animations-1/#viewtimeline-interface

The spec says a view progress timeline's source resolves as "the nearest ancestor of the subject whose principal box establishes a scroll container". For a `position: fixed` subject (or an absolutely positioned subject whose containing block chain passes through a fixed-position ancestor), such an ancestor can exist in the element tree, but no scroll container's scrolling displaces the subject relative to the viewport. The spec does not appear to clearly define what progress the timeline reports.

```html
<div style="overflow: scroll; height: 200px;">
  <div style="height: 1000px;">
    <div style="position: fixed; top: 50px; height: 100px; view-timeline: --t;">fixed</div>
  </div>
</div>
```

What is `--t`'s source, and what progress does it report as the scroller scrolls?

Presumably we do not want a fixed-position subject to resolve a source and essentially want to have an inactive timeline.

There are no WPTs covering fixed-position subjects as far as I can see.

(This seems to be related to #13988 which discusses when view timelines should be inactive on containers with no scrollable overflow.)

> Presumably we do not want a fixed-position subject to resolve a source and essentially want to have an inactive timeline.

Even without scrolling displacement an element could have different progress values in its scrollport, and that could be useful information to have for styling. And it can change its position based on other factors (dimensions of content around, window/container size, etc.)

And indeed, this is similar to https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13988, and most examples from it would apply for fixpos as well.

## @jwatt — 2026-08-07

Good point. I think the spec still needs to define what the [ranges](https://drafts.csswg.org/scroll-animations-1/#view-timelines-ranges) mean in this scenario though. Some possibilities(?):

- read the "earliest"/"latest" definitions literally: the intersection condition holds at every scroll offset (or none), so cover clamps to the whole scroll range and progress degenerates to roughly `scroll()`.
- compute the ranges from the subject's viewport-relative position as if it were content at scroll offset zero
- compute the ranges from the subject's hypothetical static position (analogous to how sticky subjects should use the un-stuck position; annoying for subjects nested inside fixed subtrees?)
