In #10912 we resolved that in the [scroll marker focus order](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-overflow-5/#focus-order) the scroll marker group scroll markers come either before or after the content depending on the position specified in the `scroll-marker-group` property. However, informed by a11y expert feedback in the chromium implementation they are always focused before.

The rationale is that scroll markers should be placed first in the tab order, even if they are visually rendered after the content in the layout, because it gives keyboard and assistive technology (AT) users immediate access to the primary navigation and controlling affordances right away. Accessibility review supported this approach as the more useful experience for users.

Proposed resolution:
The [::scroll-marker-group](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-overflow-5/#selectordef-scroll-marker-group) pseudo-elements of the [scroll container](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-overflow-3/#scroll-container) are focused first regardless of the `scroll-marker-group` property value.

While trying this out in Chrome Canary in [this demo](https://codepen.io/editor/bramus/pen/01a0571c-931b-743f-a352-476408feeec9), this “markers get tabbed first”-behavior left me very much confused, as I would have expect tabbing to follow the order of how things visually appear.

In the demo, try setting the scroll markers placement to `after` so that they visually appear after the contents. As you then tab around, they get tabbed into first — before the carousel’s contents, which felt very confusing to me.

>  The scroll-marker group is placed after the slides using `scroll-marker-group: after`, so its markers should follow the scroll buttons in focus order.

## @sturobson — 2026-10-14

As a sighted user that uses a keyboard to move through the page, my expectation is that the tab order would follow the visual flow of the page. When the scroll markers sit after the previous/next buttons visually, so reaching them earlier in the tab sequence contradicts the visual hierarchy. 

I think that's a mismatch with the logical focus order — the order should preserve the page's meaning, and the visual flow is the clearest expression of that meaning - which is a behaviour that WCAG 2.4.3 (Focus Order), I believe, is designed to prevent.
