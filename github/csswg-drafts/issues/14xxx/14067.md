In #12665 and #13668, we resolved that inside a line-clamp container, floats are clipped to the block-end content edge of the container. But it's not clear how that should work when the container has a `border-radius`.

Per css-backgrounds, a border-radius causes the content edge to also have a radius. But radii apply to corners, not edges. Since there's no clipping on the inline-start or -end edges, if we apply the radius to the block-end edge, where does the curve stop?

It's probably better to ignore the radius and treat the edge as an infinite horizontal line.

## @frivoal — 2026-08-11

Reopening: the question here was essentially: "shaped or unshaped content edge?" We resolved to do the same as `overflow-y:clip`. However:
* `overflow-y: clip` is defined to apply at the padding edge by default (though you can change that), while we had [previously resolved](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/12665#issuecomment-3303665916) that float clipping inside line-clamp should specifically be at the content edge
* The demos shown during the discussion of this issue showed attempts at following the shaped edge, and it seemed to me the general reaction was "that's silly, let's not do that, and instead follow what `overflow-y: clip` does", but overflow-y is [defined](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-overflow-3/#overflow-clip-edge) to use a shaped edge, and I am not sure we were very clear on that. Maybe we did mean to have the same behavior, whatever it is, to keep things consistent, but if we meant, as I felt during the discussion, to keep it simple by not dealing with the shape, then we got the resolution wrong.
* We do not seem to have interop on overflow-clip in 1D with a shaped edge (`overflow-y` but not `-x`). [Here's an example with](https://software.hixie.ch/utilities/js/live-dom-viewer/?saved=14817) float with a negative left margin, extending out of a container with rounded corners:
  | Firefox | Safari | Chrome |
  |-|-|-|
  | <img width="290" height="290" alt="Firefox clips to the shaped padding edge in 2d before applying the negative margin, then shifts horizontally without clipping" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/7edcdcc5-0477-49b4-a764-ae4453e06823" /> | <img width="290" height="290" alt="Safari clips to the shaped padding edge in 2d" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/a36a56d1-9333-4fd9-9fa4-460e5512f317" /> | <img width="290" height="290" alt="Chrome clips to the unshaped padding edge vertically, and doesn't clip horizontally" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/498776b8-c9cd-449f-b627-4c6240809980" /> |


So, do we want to:
1. Confirm the resolution we just passed, and stick to the same clipping behavior as overflow clip, at the block-end edge, even though (a) that's not the content edge we previously resolved on, and (b) there isn't yet implemented agreement as to what actually does in practice?
2. Undo this last resolution, and clip at the unshaped content edge, or order to (a) stick with the content edge as previously resolved, and (b) keep it simple in terms of edge shaping?
3. Something else?

I wouldn't object either way, but my preference is 2.
* I think we should stick to the content edge, because that's where in-flow content gets clamped, so we should follow that
* I think we should use the unshaped edge, because anything else is complicated, and I am unconvinced that we have use-cases that call for that complexity (outside of use-cases that call for the full complexity of `overflow: discard`).

## @frivoal — 2026-08-11

Oops, went a bit too fast. While the spec does define the overflow-clip-edge to be a shaped edge, it also does say:
> However, when one of [overflow-x](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-overflow-3/#propdef-overflow-x) or [overflow-y](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-overflow-3/#propdef-overflow-y) computes to [clip](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-overflow-3/#valdef-overflow-clip) and the other computes to [visible](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-overflow-3/#valdef-overflow-visible), the clipping region is not rounded.

So out of the 3 behaviors shown above, chrome is the one following the spec.

Assuming we're happy with that, there remains the narrower question of whether "do as overflow-y: clip" was supposed to only mean that we want to follow the unshaped edge, or whether we actually also did intend to switch from the content edge to the padding edge.

For now, given that we do have an explicit resolution about the content edge, I'll make spec edits based on the understanding that we stick to the edge we had previously resolved on, and that it was just a round-about way to say "unshaped edge". However, I'm still putting this back on the agenda to confirm.
