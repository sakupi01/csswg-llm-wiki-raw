Splitting out from https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14254 (see also https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14252) ...

The WebKit customizable select demo styles adopted a slight lightening of the background color in dark modes, to make form controls feel more interactive.

```
select, input, textarea {
	background: light-dark(transparent, alpha(white / 4%));
}
```

We're proposing to adopt this into the default stylesheet.

<!-- comment id=5598495641 author=stubbornella created=2026-09-09T08:09:56Z url=https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14461#issuecomment-5598495641 -->
## @stubbornella — 2026-09-09

This isn't decorative, it is about communicating importance to the user.

Humans have evolved to pay more attention to stuff that is nearby and less to stuff that is farther away. Think speeding car a mile away versus a speeding car entering the sidewalk you are crossing. 

This applies to web design because we need to make the most important things look closer to the viewer. In light mode, you achieve this by casting a drop shadow from the picker and using a thin border around the buttons, inputs, and selects. That won't have the same effect in dark mode, because cast shadows and subtle borders are close to invisible. Instead, to communicate importance in dark mode, you need to lighten the background color. Lighter stuff appears closer to the user, darker panels recede. 

To achieve this, we need to take 2 steps:
1. Slightly lighten all form control backgrounds
2. More significantly lighten backgrounds in the top layer, like pickers, because they are the _most important_ thing on the page **while they are open**.

In this image you can see the effect, the inputs are lighter and the picker is even more so.

## @fantasai — 2026-09-12

See https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14318 wrt forced colors mode. Wrt `currentBackgroundColor`, that's not going to have a lightening effect. (It's actually very unpredictable what it might do.)

We've got basically three options that make sense, two of which are technically equivalent:

- Use `white` or `CanvasText` (which resolves to `white` in dark modes). This will always lighten the background.
- Use `currentColor`. This might darken the background, if the control has dark text on a light background (which it might even if the overall `color-scheme` is dark).
