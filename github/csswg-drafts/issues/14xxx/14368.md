### The problem

The update callback exists so developers can delay the transition until the new DOM is ready. That's hard to do for **nested iframes**: if you insert an iframe and then resolve the callback, the iframe almost certainly hasn't loaded or produced a frame, so the transition animates a blank rectangle.

The browser could help by internally delaying the animation phase (with a timeout) until nested iframes have produced a frame.

### Two unresolved sub-problems

**1. A circularity that was never answered.** @smaug---- pointed out in the original thread that the difficulty isn't network load, it's *painting*: if rendering is suppressed in the embedder during the transition, when does rAF start ticking in the iframe — i.e. how can the iframe ever produce the frame we're waiting for? This now interacts directly with [rendering suppression for view transitions](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-view-transitions-2/#document-rendering-suppression-for-view-transitions), which is normatively defined. @jakearchibald's follow-up question — whether browsers already have a workaround for the analogous case of an iframe participating in a CSS animation — was never answered either.

**2. A cross-origin timing leak.** Delaying the animation until a cross-origin iframe has painted lets the embedder measure that iframe's readiness, which it otherwise can't. Same-origin iframes seem fine; cross-origin needs a mitigation or an explicit decision not to wait.

### Possible framing

Since this was filed, HTML gained render-blocking machinery that may be the right vocabulary here:

- whatwg/html#10513 — adding `view-transition` as a token for `blocking`
- whatwg/html#12704 — a `Document` becoming render-blocking during "update the rendering"

If the answer is "the embedder waits, subject to a timeout, and only for same-origin frames", expressing that in terms of render-blocking rather than a bespoke VT rule seems preferable.
