§10.9 lists five forms of one color as exactly equivalent: `#7654CD`, `rgb(46.27% 32.94% 80.39%)`, `lab(44.36% 36.05 -58.99)`, `color(xyz-d50 0.2005 0.14089 0.4472)` and `color(xyz-d65 0.21661 0.14602 0.59452)`.

The two XYZ values are not those of #7654CD. They follow from the already rounded values: the xyz-d50 is `lab(44.36% 36.05 -58.99)` converted and rounded, and the xyz-d65 is that rounded xyz-d50 adapted to D65.

| | printed | computed from #7654CD |
|---|---|---|
| xyz-d50 | 0.2005 0.14089 0.4472 | 0.20049 0.14087 0.44708 |
| xyz-d65 | 0.21661 0.14602 0.59452 | 0.21660 0.14600 0.59437 |

It is harmless on screen: the printed XYZ still round to #7654CD (117.98, 84.01, 205.02). It only shows when the example is used as a test value, as I did: Z of the xyz-d65 form is off by 1.5e-4.

Suggested fix: `color(xyz-d50 0.20049 0.14087 0.44708)` and `color(xyz-d65 0.21660 0.14600 0.59437)`.

Computed with the §19 sample code's matrices and with colorjs.io 0.7.1; culori 4.0.2 and the color-space npm library, which I maintain, agree to the digits shown.
