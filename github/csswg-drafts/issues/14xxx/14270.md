For #14142, I had to handle the `outset-adjusted border radius` for non-round corner shapes.

Basically, I'm extracting the adjusted outset from `outset-adjusted border radius` (because `adjustedRadius = radius + adjustedOutset`) and drawing the corner as the offset of a virtual corner with the same radius as the original corner, positioned at a distance of `adjustedOutset` from the outset rectangle (target rect).

I would like feedback on whether my current approach (`adjustedOutset = adjustedRadius - radius`) is acceptable, or whether another generalization would make more sense.

The compatibility requirements are that `round` corners keep their current outset-adjusted behavior and that the transition between `round` corners and other corner shapes is continuous.

As a side note: I'm not sure "adjusted radius" is a good geometric abstraction for arbitrary corner shapes. The algorithm inside `outset-adjusted border radius` was likely designed around round corners and does not necessarily generalize well to other shapes.

## @sb3nder — 2026-09-26

I think whatever we do for round corners we should do for every `corner-shape` to keep the corner size consistent.
I'm proposing:

Subtract radius from adjustedRadius to get adjustedOutset, and draw the corner as an offset corner positioned at a distance of adjustedOutset from the target rectangle.

Alternatively, worded using inset:
Subtract adjustedRadius from radius to get adjustedInset, and draw the corner as an offset corner positioned at a distance of adjustedInset from the target rectangle.
