The specific [WICG Aspect Ratio Incubator Project](https://github.com/WICG/aspect-ratio) was closed, and its README.md now points readers to this issue. I'm not sure why that happened, since Pixel Aspect Ratio isn't limited to CSS.

I'm trying to draw attention to the fact that [Pixel Aspect Ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixel_aspect_ratio) (PAR) is still not rendered correctly by any of the current browsers I've tested. PAR is a piece of metadata embedded in JPG, PNG, and GIF files.

But a large amount of content from the '80s and '90s used rectangular pixels, and most of them with **fractional ratios**. IOW, simple pixel duplication cannot reproduce the correct aspect.

A lot of legacy pixel art based on rectangular pixels (*1) is being effectively destroyed, since this use case is still alive, and users are working around it by artificially stretching images via resize to get the desired aspect ratio. Once an image has been stretched this way, it's nearly impossible to restore it losslessly.

The same problem affects all stills and video captured from SDTV. NTSC had an effective resolution of 720×480, meaning its pixels are not square (same deal with PAL, but at 720x576). Just like with pixel art, users have no option but to artificially stretch the image to fit square pixels, introducing artifacts that result in permanent image quality loss.

Inspired by the [HTML Acid Tests](https://www.acidtests.org), I created the Pixel Aspect Ratio Acid Test, linked below. It checks whether a browser renders two sets of images correctly.

http://frs.badcoffee.info/PAR_AcidTest/

*1: Think C64, CGA, EGA, VGA (320×200), SNES, MSX, Amiga, TurboGrafx-16, NEC PC-88, and most [devices from the '80s](https://pineight.com/mw/page/Dot_clock_rates.xhtml), when square pixels were still an expensive luxury.
