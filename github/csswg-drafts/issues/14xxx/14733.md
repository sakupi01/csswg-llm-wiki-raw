An interesting bug report was filed on WebKit earlier this year: [WebKit bug 310210](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=310210). The originator of that bug reports that on his website, which uses view timeline with the root scroller as their source to animate tiles in as you scroll, the tiles would not animate right as they entered the bottom of the display but only after they had cleared the "obscured insets" area of Safari on iPhone.

Indeed, Safari on iPhone may present application Chrome at the bottom of the display, in the area that incidentally is where the display is rounded. As a result the page's root scroller roughly considers the rectangular part of the display to the scrollable area and the parts above and below that as that "obscured insets" area.

As such, while the tiles are within that "obscured insets" area and not yet in the "safe" (ie. rectangular) area, their progress is unresolved and the underlying value is used since those animations don't use a backwards or forwards fill – which would avoid the issue.

I think as it stands, this is a reasonable behavior to support, but this surprised the developer negatively. This is why I'm filing this issue to see if we should have dedicated logic for scrollers which don't simply clip content to the scrollable area but may present parts of the scrollable content outside of it.

To help visualize what I wrote, you can check out the [website in question](https://neiltonge.co.uk/gallery/collections/2025) and a [screen recording showing the issue](https://bug-310210-attachments.webkit.org/attachment.cgi?id=478717).
