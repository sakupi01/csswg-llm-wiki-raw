Check out [this test-case](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/attachment.cgi?id=9625728) from [Mozilla bug 2061066](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2061066) on a browser with classic scrollbars.

Behaviors currently are:

 * Chrome: backdrop covering the scrollbar gutter, iframe constrained by the gutter.
 * Safari: backdrop constrained by the scrollbar gutter, iframe covering the gutter.
 * Gecko: backdrop and iframe both constrained by the gutter.

The Gecko behavior is easy to explain, because both the `<iframe>` and `::backdrop` are fixed-positioned elements and we resolved that their containing-block accounted for scrollbar-gutter. All browsers seem to agree on the "regular" fixed-pos case.

I'm not quite sure what Blink and WebKit are doing but clearly they're inconsistent in general, and with each other. I _think_ Gecko's is the correct behavior per spec.

However, for this particular case, it seems somewhat unfortunate? If you're full-screening a subdocument, being constrained by the scrollbar gutters of the ancestor document seems rather unfortunate. I don't think there's an easy way to express it in CSS but maybe we could define that `iframe:fullscreen` and `iframe:fullscreen::backdrop` do _not_ get constrained by `scrollbar-gutter` if they are fixed-pos? It should probably also apply to `<object>` / embed and the like...

Am I missing something that explains either the WebKit or the Blink behavior, spec wise?
